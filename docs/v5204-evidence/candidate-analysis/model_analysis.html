Limitations

  • Retrospective descriptive research decision support; no clinical validation or guaranteed future accuracy.
  • Tuning data support model selection; calibration data fit transforms/intervals; only test data provide the untouched held-out estimate. Test inspection can influence subsequent experiments.
  • Metrics are recomputed from every stored prediction. Saved aggregate metrics are parsed and hashed but are not treated as evidence of accuracy.
  • Current registry snapshots may contain post-start updates and leakage. Completed-only and convenience samples can create selection bias; chronological splits do not establish prospective validity.
  • No regression accuracy percentage is defined. Duration errors use days. Binary decisions use probability >= 0.5; confusion matrices have actual rows and predicted columns.
  • Undefined values are null, displayed as unavailable. One-class splits do not identify class discrimination. Small held-out samples and imbalance can make estimates unstable; no uncertainty interval is invented.
  • Reliability uses ten equal-width bins. Raw and post-hoc calibrated probabilities are analyzed separately; calibration-cohort diagnostics can be optimistic.
  • Duration-by-phase subgroup metrics cannot be recomputed because saved duration prediction rows do not contain verified phase labels; stored subgroup aggregates are not substituted.
  • Charts show all stored points (overlapping points can obscure sample counts); metrics and bin counts include every row.
  • Tuning validation metrics are optimistic after hyperparameter and raw model selection; test metrics are held out. Calibration diagnostics are in-sample for the calibration transform.
  • Bootstrap intervals are conditional on the fitted models and assume independent trials; they exclude model-fitting uncertainty and sponsor/site clustering.
  • Primary probabilities and classification metrics remain raw. Separate sigmoid-calibrated probabilities/metrics exist only with at least 10 calibration rows and two labels of each class; calibration never selects models.
  • Split-conformal nominal marginal coverage assumes exchangeability, which chronological shift may violate. Finite-sample ranks beyond calibration size produce unbounded intervals; descriptive test coverage is not a future guarantee.
  • Training range/category indicators describe extrapolation and cannot estimate an OOD probability or guarantee validity.
  • Nonlinear structured branches and linear modalities share retained cohorts, but have different capacities. Additional complexity does not guarantee improved performance.
  • Tree impurity importances summarize global behavior, may favor high-cardinality features, and are neither trial-specific SHAP values nor causal evidence.
  • Text uses TF-IDF; BioBERT, survival, and SHAP are optional separate capabilities and are not claimed as executed.
  • Completed-only analysis cannot estimate censored/ongoing trial outcomes without a separate survival cohort.
  • Per-phase test metrics are exploratory; small or one-class subgroups may be unstable or undefined. Scatter plots retain at most 200 chronological rows; metrics and reliability bins use every retained row.
  • Research decision support only. Completed-only cohort creates follow-up/selection bias; minimum sample guard is not evidence of scientific validity.
